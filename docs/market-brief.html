Signoff · market brief · Flip the Switch Hackathon, Nov 2026

Agents can watch the data. People still have to own the decision.

Signoff is a pattern, shown on Citi Bike rebalancing. Agents detect data incidents and prepare the fix in the team's chat. A named owner signs off in the same thread. The decisions that depend on the data are gated on the outcome. Switch supplies the room, the roles and the sign-off gate.

Who feels it

Any team that acts on operational data within minutes, not days: micromobility and fleet operations (rebalancing, charging, dispatch), logistics (slotting, routing), fintech operations (reconciliation, payouts), retail (pricing, inventory), energy and utilities (outage and load data). The pattern is always the same: a feed or table goes bad, an alert reaches an engineer's channel, and the people deciding keep deciding on it.

Citi Bike alone ran 46.6 million rides in 2025 across ~2,500 stations. Its rebalancing optimisation was published at AAAI 2015 and put into daily use. GBFS, the feed format, is published by 1,000+ shared-mobility systems worldwide.

How it could make money

  • Open core on Switch. The rooms, roles and agents are free and self-hosted. A paid tier adds connectors (warehouses, dbt, Snowflake), two-person sign-off, rule expiry and audit export.
  • Per data owner, not per seat. Value scales with the number of datasets that have a named owner and a gate.
  • Wedge: operations teams in mobility and logistics, where a wrong decision has a visible cost (a truck, a crew, a missed delivery) within the hour.
The bet: the next data-quality product isn't a better alert. It puts the incident, the evidence, the decision and the person who decides in the room where the work happens, and makes every consumer of the data respect the outcome.
